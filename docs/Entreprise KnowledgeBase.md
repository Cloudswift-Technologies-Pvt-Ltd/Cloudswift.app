overview

An enterprise knowledge agent is an AI which employees can query using ordinary language and which then provides accurate answers by drawing on the organisation's real internal knowledge, its policies, its documentation, previous decisions and product data rather than making up responses. CloudSwift is in charge of designing, constructing and integrating this AI knowledge agent, including the implementation of appropriate access controls so that only the right people can see only the information that is relevant to them.

Instead of having traditional enterprise content management systems store and organize documents, an enterprise knowledge agent is positioned on top of that layer and actually responds to questions posed to it. It's more in the nature of a next-generation kind of knowledge management platform than simply a static repository.

Business challenges

In large organisations knowledge is spread out over wikis, shared drives, ticketing systems, Slack conversations and the heads of a small number of long-serving employees. It takes new employees longer to get up to speed since they don't know where to look. More time is lost by experienced staff members having to answer the same questions repeatedly. Documents become out of date and no one notices until somebody acts on the outdated information.

It is a typical failure mode of old-style knowledge-based systems since they keep information without making it easy to locate. Traditional keyword searches within internal tools generally end up giving the wrong document or ten documents without arriving at a clear answer. That is precisely the gap which enterprise knowledge management projects are intended to overcome and where many of them end up coming to a standstill.

what is it

An enterprise knowledge agent is an AI system that has been designed for a specific purpose and is linked to your internal knowledge resources via retrieval (RAG), which means its answers are based on the information that your organization actually has documented, rather than on general knowledge from the internet.

It has access controls so that it only displays information which a particular employee is allowed to see, and it includes escalation procedures so that it identifies gaps where documentation is missing rather than simply making an assumption.

At work, this generally makes use of agentic RAG, a type of retrieval method in which the agent chooses what to search for and how to combine the various sources. It can also, in some cases, make use of a knowledge graph to aid AI agent reasoning by linking together related documents and decisions rather than treating each source individually.

Some vendors refer to it merely as a knowledge base AI agent, whereas CloudSwift incorporates it into a more comprehensive and regulated enterprise AI solutions strategy rather than just providing an AI search function.

Industries

For SaaS and technology companies, frequently updated product documentation is naturally suited to enterprise AI solutions that have to keep up with regular releases.

Illustrative example

The example given is illustrative and does not refer to a particular customer engagement.

An enterprise knowledge agent could be connected to the company's policies and product documentation, which are distributed across a wiki, a shared drive and years of Slack threads.

Employees can put in a simple language question—for example, one about a policy detail or a product specification—and then immediately receive an accurate answer that has been approved, rather than having to search through a number of different tools or interrupt a colleague.

If there are questions that an agent is unable to answer, these can be recorded as documentation gaps for the team to deal with, thus turning what is currently a passive enterprise content management archive into a resource that employees actually use.

faqs
1.	What is an enterprise knowledge agent?
An AI system connected to your internal documentation and systems that gives employees accurate, governed answers to their questions.
2.	How is an enterprise knowledge agent different from enterprise content management software?
Enterprise content management stores, organizes and version controls your documents. A knowledge agent sits on top of that layer and actually answers questions from it, using retrieval to find and combine relevant information instead of just storing it.
3.	How is this different from a regular internal search tool?
It understands natural language questions and retrieves grounded answers directly, rather than just returning a list of documents for employees to search through.
4.	Will it expose sensitive information to the wrong people?
Its built with access controls that respect your existing permissions, so employees only see what they are authorized to see.
5.	What happens if it doesnt know an answer?
It flags the gap rather then guessing. This can also show where your documentation needs improvement.
6.	Does it replace our wiki or document management system?
No, it integrates with your existing systems rather than replacing them.
7.	How long does implementation take?
Timeline depends on the number and complexity of your knowledge sources and is scoped during discovery.
